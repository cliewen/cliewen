# P-023 — Cliewen challenges a plan before committing to it, and keeps what the work teaches

Cliewen proves that what was built matches what was stated. It has never had a way to ask whether the statement was worth making. A criterion, its implementation, and its test are typically written by one author in one sitting, so a single wrong assumption travels through all three and emerges looking verified; scenario-to-test alignment is alignment, not evidence that anyone wanted the scenario. [G-014](../goals/G-014-riskiest-assumption-is-challenged.md) is that gap, and it is the cheaper one to close, because an assumption caught while a plan is a proposal costs a paragraph and the same assumption caught at acceptance costs the campaign.

The second gap is what a change learns on the way. Operational knowledge — which command form actually works here, what the recovery is when a check rejects a candidate — is neither product meaning nor architecture nor a future-shaping decision, so the corpus has nowhere to put it and drops it when the change ends. A human rediscovers it occasionally; an agent rediscovers it every session, because a fresh context is the normal case. [G-015](../goals/G-015-what-the-work-teaches-is-retained.md) is that gap, and its failure mode is the opposite of the first: a capture obligation with no eligibility bar produces a folder of near-duplicate notes that costs more to read than the rediscovery it prevents, and writing down a workaround is how a workaround becomes permanent.

The two belong in one campaign because the campaign's own guides proposal is exactly the kind of consequential commitment G-014 says to challenge before making. [AN-024](../analysis/AN-024-methodology-review-acceptance-and-learning.md) noticed that and recommended it as the first delivery for that reason: the learning mechanism is the challenge rule's first real test subject, and if the challenge rule cannot find anything to push back on in a proposal the maintainer already likes, it is not doing anything.

What this campaign must not do is ship either rule before it has worked. Every rule here is a methodology rule, and a methodology rule written onto a carrier under `internal/skills/source/` reaches every adopter at the next release, where retracting it costs a second full change against repositories that have already started following it. So M-094 through M-098 bind this repository only, and M-100 is the single milestone that moves proven text onto the shipped carriers — or records why it should not. An obligation this repository could not itself sustain is not one to export.

M-099 belongs to [G-017](../goals/G-017-the-method-is-evidenced-and-simplified.md) rather than to this campaign's own result. It is here because baseline observation that starts after the changes it was meant to measure is not a baseline, and AN-024 says so explicitly. It is the one milestone whose value depends on starting early rather than finishing well.

Milestone numbering continues corpus-global numbering from P-022's M-093, allocated through the coordinated ledger CH-182 enabled.
