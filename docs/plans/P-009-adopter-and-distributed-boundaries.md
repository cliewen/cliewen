# P-009 — Cliewen prices what adoption and distributed work actually need

P-008 closed having answered two interface questions with adopter evidence instead of design: [AN-012](../analysis/AN-012-adopter-configuration-cost.md) found a real adopter's actual configuration needs were a corpus wall it had to fork and an upgrade path it could not follow, not a configuration file; [AN-013](../analysis/AN-013-distributed-work-and-evidence-boundaries.md) reproduced that no artifact records a dependent change's base or authorization, that cross-repository evidence is discharged by hand, and that no foreign identifier can be expressed at all. Both analyses ended without an interface — M-035 and M-036 forbade implementing one — and each named this campaign as the consumer of its routed candidates. [G-001](../goals/G-001-verifiable-thread.md)'s thread stays verifiable only if the interfaces Cliewen eventually ships trace back to measured need, not speculative design.

This campaign takes the four candidates both analyses marked load-bearing, in the order their dependencies suggest: the two process and corpus-format decisions first, because they touch no adopter-facing surface and nothing else in this plan depends on them being unsettled; the two adopter-facing distribution decisions after, because both become public contracts the moment an adopted repository depends on them, and the corpus-upgrade milestone assumes the format decisions ahead of it are already stable. Every milestone is a separate full Cliewen change rooted at accepted `main`, published as a ready pull request, and accepted by human merge before the next begins. Milestone numbering continues corpus-global numbering from P-008 (M-037…M-040).

## Explicitly out of this campaign

AN-013 candidate 3 (external references are qualified — `owner/repo#N` or a full URL — and a bare forge number becomes a lint failure, repairing the 48 unqualified references AN-013 found) is not a dedicated milestone: it may ride any change that already touches the corpus, and this plan does not reserve it. AN-013 candidate 4 (ADR-009's delegated JVM per-test purpose carrier either becomes something extraction demonstrably installs, or the delegation is withdrawn) belongs with whatever change next revisits extraction, not with this campaign's adoption-and-distribution scope. AN-012 candidate 3 (a release that changes corpus obligations states its migration to an adopter) and candidate 4 (`guide/operations.md`'s stale `.github/tools/` upgrade procedure) are not dedicated milestones either; candidate 4 may ride any change touching the guide, and candidate 3 is left for whichever future change first needs it. AN-013's finding about the pinned adopter's own JVM suites and hand-maintained wall fork, and AN-012's finding about that same adopter's absent acceptance-brief gate, are the adopter's decisions, not Cliewen's, and stay out of scope here except as the case M-039 re-evaluates. This campaign does not reopen ADR-013's rejection of a `cliewen.yaml` configuration file — AN-012 measured no unmet need for one — and does not promise that M-037 or M-038's design survives contact with implementation; each milestone's own change may find a cheaper or different shape than its exit criterion assumes, subject to the plan's mutation rules below.

## Mutation rules

Status and evidence fields in the milestone table may mutate in an implementing change's merge digest. Everything else changes only through a declared plan revision backed by a decision record routed by reversal cost. Plan adjustments are decisions.
