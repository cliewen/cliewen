# P-007 — Cliewen hardens its core

P-006 digested the first adoption; this campaign digests the first critiques. Two independent reviews received 2026-07-25 — a methodology-level review and an enterprise migration assessment — reduce to four patterns recorded in [AN-008](../analysis/AN-008-methodology-critiques.md): the human half of the merge boundary is empty, the judge proves less than the skill prose promises, the graph only accumulates, and the whole loop is priced for the authoring repositories. Each pattern is a half-built face of a core element ([ARCH-003](../architecture/core.md)), so the campaign works core-first: the human gate, then judge honesty, then a corpus that can forget, then proportionate cost. It serves [G-001](../goals/G-001-verifiable-thread.md) — a thread whose human end is unstated and whose green is weaker than its claim is not verifiable in the sense the goal means.

Where a design already exists in the Intent Engineering book that Cliewen is born from — the after-gate human checklist, the coverage-pair convention with its single-direction exemption, the per-scenario test-type declaration, the spec-first review order — the milestone imports it rather than reinventing it, keeping Cliewen and the book coherent.

The campaign is sequential. Each milestone is a separate full Cliewen change rooted at accepted `main`, published as a ready pull request, and accepted by human merge before the next begins. A milestone that changes what the judge checks, what the review loop does, or what the merge gate asks also updates every guide page that states the old limit in the same change — the guide is the published face of the same rules, and a closed gap still described as open is documentation drift. Milestone numbering is corpus-global and continues from P-006 (M-024…M-031). M-024 through M-028 alter what a merge accepts or what a green validate asserts, so each is a red-line change under [C-013](../constraints/C-013-core-changes-need-decision.md): it carries its own explicit decision record and human acceptance, never riding silently.

M-030 and M-031 were appended by a declared plan revision ([PDR-014](../decisions/PDR-014-distribution-reopens-on-the-active-campaign.md)), reopening the package-manager distribution scope that P-004 through P-006 each parked. They are not core hardening: they touch neither the judge, the merge gate, nor the corpus graph, and they serve [C-015](../constraints/C-015-onboarding-under-30-minutes.md) by way of [CAP-001](../capabilities/CAP-001-onboarding/README.md) and [CAP-004](../capabilities/CAP-004-ship/README.md). They ride here because this campaign's exclusion list, alone among the campaigns, never named installation, and because a successor plan would put six sequential red-line milestones in front of a measured gap at the front door. They stay ordered: M-031 installs what M-030 publishes.

## Explicitly out of this campaign

Adopter hardening that a real pilot must price first: CI-wall configurability (runner labels, pinned actions, binary location), a non-destructive extraction rehearsal pass, a blessed local-extension point beside managed skills, a configurable skills-mirror location, multi-prefix extraction handling, stacked or dependent changes, cross-repository evidence, and external-tracker metadata conventions. Also out: the production feedback loop beyond M-028's incident convention (the V3 door stays shut), any change to the book itself, changes inside adopting repositories, and reopening P-006's closed findings. Named here so it is not lost, but out of this campaign: foreign documentation kinds beyond [ADR-026](../decisions/ADR-026-adopter-types-default-lifecycle.md)'s open type system — form-checking a foreign kind's content, natively or by delegating to the kind's own validator, and keeping it fresh through the ordinary change loop (C4 diagrams and FINOS CALM models are illustrative candidates, not commitments); this is a candidate goal to propose in its own change, and the plan that pursues it should follow a real adopter's mandate rather than precede one.
