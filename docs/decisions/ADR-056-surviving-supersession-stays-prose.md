# ADR-056 — A live superseded decision stays prose a second time

## Context and problem statement

[PDR-038](PDR-038-supersession-residue-declined.md) declined widening `supersedes:` to a decision record that is superseded but survives, and routed the question to P-013's successor campaign, [P-014](../plans/P-014-supersession-edge.md), because a simplification campaign under [PDR-026](PDR-026-campaigns-close-on-re-derived-evidence.md) may not add machinery to argue for itself. P-014's M-069 exists to make the argument on its own terms rather than assume the door leads to *yes*. [ADR-034](ADR-034-retirement-is-deletion.md) binds `supersedes:` to deletion: an ID named there must no longer resolve to a live artifact. [AN-022](../analysis/AN-022-remaining-surface-scored.md) counted nine of the corpus's then eighty-four decision index rows stating a live supersession or amendment relationship in prose, because the decision each names is exactly the kind that must stay readable: PDR-001 lives on, superseded by PDR-004; PDR-003 lives on, superseded by PDR-006; and so on. That count is history, not a live figure — the corpus has grown to ninety rows at this change's head, and the same pattern has recurred twice more in the interim without anyone deciding anything, because [PDR-037](PDR-037-tooling-is-judged-by-what-it-holds.md) amends [PDR-029](PDR-029-simplification-tests-by-surface.md) and [PDR-040](PDR-040-push-is-durability-ready-is-explicit.md) amends four PDRs at once, each stating its own relationship in prose the same way the original nine did. The milestone's subject is AN-022's original nine specifically, not a fresh census of every row the pattern has since touched. The milestone asks for a decision that either widens the field to that case or declines it a second time with a fuller argument than the first, stating three things the first decline did not: the obligation a superseding change would gain, how `clue validate` would tell a live superseded record from a stale one, and whether the reverse question — what was downstream of a decision — gets answered.

## Decision outcome

**The widening is declined a second time. `supersedes:` keeps ADR-034's meaning exactly, a live supersession or amendment stays prose, and the one row among the nine that did not already state its claim in the settled shape is corrected so the shape is uniform.**

- **The obligation a superseding change would gain.** Today, superseding a live decision costs one sentence on each side: the new record's body says what it changes, and the old record's index row says who changed it — no schema, no field, no check. Widening `supersedes:` to a survivor would add, to every future superseding change: a frontmatter entry naming the superseded ID; whatever the superseded record's own frontmatter would need to carry a matching edge back, since the field as designed lives only on the successor; and the obligation to keep both current if a third decision later re-supersedes either one, a chain nothing tracks today because nothing has needed it to. That cost lands on every future author of a superseding decision, not only on the nine records this spike already found.
- **How `clue validate` would tell a live superseded record from a stale one.** `checkSupersedes`'s existing check, for the deletion case, is purely existential: does the named ID still resolve to a live artifact? That question is answerable because deletion is an observable fact — the file is there, or it is not. A live-superseded edge has no equivalent fact to observe. Both records exist and both remain `verified`; "the edge is still accurate" is a claim about whether the superseding record's prose still correctly describes what it changed, which is exactly the kind of judgment ADR-034 built `checkSupersedes` to avoid needing. A rule for the survivor case could confirm the pointer resolves to a live artifact — which is true of every fresh edge and every stale one alike — but not that the pointer remains true. A widened field's check would pass on a stale edge exactly as readily as on a fresh one, so it would be checked in name only.
- **Whether the reverse question gets answered.** Pattern C's motivating question is *what was downstream of this decision*, asked from the superseded record looking forward. A `supersedes:` entry, widened or not, is carried by the superseding record and points backward at what it replaced. Asking PDR-001 "what superseded you" still needs a walk over every other decision's `supersedes:` list, and [PDR-034](PDR-034-the-corpus-is-read-narrowly-by-default.md) makes exactly that reverse walk the thing `clue context` deliberately does not do. Widening the field would not answer the question that motivates it; answering it needs a second, generated backward index — machinery beyond the field itself, and machinery a decline is not the place to design.

Those three costs are each a standing addition — a per-change authoring obligation, a check that cannot discriminate the one thing it would exist to discriminate, and a question the field alone does not answer — set against nine records that already answer a human reader today, in the format every other decision in this corpus already uses: prose. The gap is real and it is exactly nine rows wide; the mechanism proportional to closing it is not smaller than what widening would cost, and PDR-038's first decline understated the second and third costs by naming them as open risks rather than showing why each one fails on its own terms.

**The settled shape.** A decision index row states a live supersession or amendment claim using a paired, active verb: the older record's row reads `superseded by <ID>[, <clause>]` (or, for a partial revision, `amended by <ID>[, <clause>]`); the newer record's row reads `supersedes <ID>[, <clause>]` (or `amends <ID>[, <clause>]`). A row long enough to carry a full descriptive sentence under [ADR-046](ADR-046-index-rows-say-what-the-artifact-is-about.md) states the same relationship in ordinary prose instead of the short trailing form; both are the settled shape, because ADR-046 already governs how a full descriptive row reads and this decision does not reopen that. Eight of AN-022's nine rows already used this shape without anyone having named it — including [PDR-031](PDR-031-architecture-artifacts-are-traces.md)'s own row, which amends PDR-029 in exactly the descriptive-sentence form. [ADR-019](ADR-019-init-regenerates-indexes.md)'s row did not: it stated its supersession claim inside the row's own descriptive title — "ADR-013's emits-empty clause is superseded" — without naming what superseded it, instead of the trailing `supersedes <ID>[, <clause>]` form every other superseding row uses. That row, and the frontmatter title it was generated from, are corrected in this change to match the other eight; no other row's wording or meaning changes. The two rows the corpus has grown by since AN-022's pin — PDR-037's and PDR-040's — already conform to the same shape unprompted, which is further evidence the shape was already this corpus's practice and not an invention of this record.

**Carrier:** this record; [ADR-019](ADR-019-init-regenerates-indexes.md)'s corrected index row; and a new sentence in `docs/decisions/README.md`'s preamble, beside ADR-034's, naming that a live-but-superseded relationship stays prose. No skill, template, or CLI text states a rule about this case — PDR-038 already recorded that none do, and this decision changes nothing that would create one. The scaffolded `docs/decisions/README.md` template ships no retirement or supersession prose at all today, so it carries no claim this decision would leave stale; this record's subject is this corpus's specific nine-row residue and the wording convention its own index already mostly follows, not a generic rule an adopter's fresh, decision-free corpus would need on day one.

### Rejected: widen `supersedes:` to a decision record that is superseded but survives

This is the alternative the milestone poses as the other answer, and it is what would close pattern C's residue outright. It is rejected for the three costs stated above: it adds an authoring obligation to every future superseding change, it gives `checkSupersedes` a rule it cannot actually enforce because a live edge's staleness is not an observable fact, and it does not answer the reverse question that motivates it without further machinery this decision does not design. [PDR-029](PDR-029-simplification-tests-by-surface.md)'s shared-memory clause is not a counterargument here: declining the field removes nothing the corpus can already say, because prose already carries the fact and always has.

### Rejected: build the reverse index alongside the widened field

Answering *what was downstream* for real needs a generated backward index — a rendered "superseded by" section derived from every record's forward pointers, in the shape [ADR-054](ADR-054-derived-extraction-report-region.md) already uses for a different derived figure. That would close the third cost this decision names, but it is new machinery layered on a widened field this decision has just rejected on its own terms, to close a nine-row gap. It remains available to a future change with its own milestone, exactly the way PDR-038 already routed the narrower widening question here.

### Rejected: leave the nine rows in their previous, individually worded shapes

Declining the field does not by itself require touching the rows — nothing in eight of the nine was factually wrong, and the ninth's fact was right even though its shape was not. But leaving the shape unnamed is part of why this question read as open across two campaigns: a reader meeting nine differently worded sentences, one of them not even naming its successor in the expected place, has no way to tell that a convention exists at all. Naming the shape once and correcting the one row that did not follow it turns nine sentences into one named convention with nine instances, at the cost of a single-row edit — cheaper than either widening the field or leaving the wording to keep drifting.
